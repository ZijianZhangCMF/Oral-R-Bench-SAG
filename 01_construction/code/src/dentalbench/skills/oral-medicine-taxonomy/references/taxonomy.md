# OralBench Taxonomy — Full Reference

## Knowledge Levels

### K1: Factual Knowledge

Recall of discrete, isolated facts, terminology, definitions, or classifications.

Decision criteria — assign K1 when:
- The item can be answered by recalling a single fact, name, number, or definition.
- No clinical reasoning, synthesis, or judgment is required.
- The answer does not depend on context or patient-specific information.

Examples:
- "What is the most common site of oral squamous cell carcinoma?" → lateral border of the tongue
- "Which vitamin deficiency causes glossitis?" → B12/folate
- "What is the Nikolsky sign?"
- "Name the types of oral lichen planus."

Counter-examples (NOT K1):
- "A patient presents with glossitis. What workup would you order?" → requires procedural reasoning → K3

### K2: Conceptual Knowledge

Understanding of relationships, principles, classifications, and pathophysiological mechanisms that connect facts into coherent frameworks.

Decision criteria — assign K2 when:
- The item requires understanding why or how something occurs, not just what it is.
- The answer involves explaining a mechanism, comparing/contrasting entities, or classifying based on criteria.
- The item tests understanding of a principle applicable to novel situations.

Examples:
- "Explain the pathogenesis of oral lichen planus."
- "What distinguishes erosive from reticular lichen planus?"
- "Why are immunocompromised patients more susceptible to oral candidiasis?"
- "Classify the following lesion using the WHO classification of odontogenic tumors."

Counter-examples (NOT K2):
- "Name the types of oral lichen planus." → pure recall → K1
- "How would you manage a patient with erosive lichen planus?" → treatment planning → K3

### K3: Procedural Knowledge

Knowledge of how to perform clinical tasks — diagnostic workups, treatment protocols, surgical techniques, and step-by-step management algorithms.

Decision criteria — assign K3 when:
- The item asks what to do, how to do it, or what sequence of steps to follow.
- The answer involves a clinical protocol, technique, or decision algorithm.
- The item presents a clinical scenario requiring a management decision.

Examples:
- "Describe the steps for performing an incisional biopsy of a suspicious oral lesion."
- "A patient with pemphigus vulgaris develops new oral erosions. What is your management plan?"
- "What is the correct technique for administering an inferior alveolar nerve block?"
- "Outline the treatment protocol for Stage II oral squamous cell carcinoma."

Counter-examples (NOT K3):
- "What is an incisional biopsy?" → definition → K1
- "Why is incisional biopsy preferred over excisional biopsy for large lesions?" → conceptual reasoning → K2

### K4: Metacognitive Knowledge

Higher-order reasoning about clinical decision-making itself — recognizing uncertainty, evaluating evidence quality, identifying cognitive biases, reflecting on diagnostic reasoning, and integrating multiple knowledge domains.

Decision criteria — assign K4 when:
- The item requires evaluating the quality or applicability of evidence.
- The item asks the clinician to reflect on their own reasoning process.
- The item involves resolving conflicting information or managing diagnostic uncertainty.
- The item requires integrating knowledge across multiple domains for a complex, ambiguous case.
- The item asks about limitations of a diagnostic test or treatment approach.

Examples:
- "A systematic review shows conflicting evidence for topical tacrolimus in OLP. How would you evaluate the evidence and counsel your patient?"
- "You initially suspected oral candidiasis, but antifungal treatment failed. What cognitive biases might have influenced your initial diagnosis?"
- "A patient with multiple comorbidities presents with oral ulcers. How do you prioritize differential diagnoses when the presentation is atypical?"
- "What are the limitations of toluidine blue staining as a screening tool for oral malignancy?"

Counter-examples (NOT K4):
- "What is the sensitivity of toluidine blue staining?" → factual → K1
- "Describe how to perform toluidine blue staining." → procedural → K3

## Dimensions (D1–D10)

### D1: Disease Identification and Classification

Scope: recognizing diseases by name, classifying them within established systems (WHO, ICD), and identifying subtypes.

Assign D1 when the item asks to: name a disease, classify a lesion, identify a condition from a description, or distinguish subtypes within a disease category.

Examples:
- "Identify this lesion based on the clinical description."
- "Classify this odontogenic tumor using the 2022 WHO classification."
- "What subtype of pemphigus does this presentation represent?"

### D2: Etiology and Pathogenesis

Scope: understanding the causes, risk factors, and pathophysiological mechanisms underlying oral diseases.

Assign D2 when the item asks about: causative agents (viral, bacterial, fungal, autoimmune), risk factors (smoking, alcohol, immunosuppression), genetic predispositions, or molecular/cellular mechanisms of disease.

Examples:
- "What is the causative agent of herpangina?"
- "Describe the autoimmune mechanism in pemphigus vulgaris."
- "What risk factors predispose to oral squamous cell carcinoma?"

### D3: Clinical Presentation and Symptoms

Scope: recognizing the signs, symptoms, and clinical features of oral diseases as they present in patients.

Assign D3 when the item asks about: what a disease looks like clinically, what symptoms patients report, typical vs. atypical presentations, or clinical staging.

Examples:
- "Describe the typical clinical appearance of oral lichen planus."
- "What symptoms do patients with burning mouth syndrome report?"
- "How does the clinical presentation of erythroplakia differ from leukoplakia?"

### D4: Diagnostic Methods and Interpretation

Scope: selecting, performing, and interpreting diagnostic tests — including laboratory tests, imaging, biopsy, and clinical examination techniques.

Assign D4 when the item asks about: which test to order, how to interpret results (histopathology, blood work, imaging), sensitivity/specificity of tests, or diagnostic criteria.

Examples:
- "What histopathological features confirm a diagnosis of oral lichen planus?"
- "Interpret this panoramic radiograph showing a radiolucent lesion in the mandible."
- "Which laboratory tests help differentiate pemphigus from pemphigoid?"

### D5: Differential Diagnosis

Scope: generating and narrowing a list of possible diagnoses based on clinical findings.

Assign D5 when the item asks to: list differential diagnoses, distinguish between clinically similar conditions, or explain what features help differentiate one condition from another.

Examples:
- "List the differential diagnoses for a white patch on the buccal mucosa."
- "What clinical features distinguish oral lichen planus from lichenoid drug reaction?"
- "A patient presents with desquamative gingivitis. What are the possible diagnoses?"

### D6: Treatment Planning and Management

Scope: selecting and sequencing treatments — pharmacological, surgical, and supportive — for oral diseases.

Assign D6 when the item asks about: drug selection and dosing, surgical approaches, treatment algorithms, management of complications, or multidisciplinary care coordination.

Examples:
- "What is the first-line treatment for erosive oral lichen planus?"
- "Describe the surgical management of a mucocele."
- "How do you manage medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw?"

### D7: Prognosis and Follow-up

Scope: predicting disease outcomes, recurrence risk, and designing follow-up protocols.

Assign D7 when the item asks about: survival rates, recurrence rates, long-term outcomes, follow-up intervals, or prognostic factors.

Examples:
- "What is the 5-year survival rate for Stage II oral squamous cell carcinoma?"
- "What is the recurrence rate of pleomorphic adenoma after surgical excision?"
- "How frequently should a patient with oral leukoplakia be followed?"

### D8: Prevention and Patient Education

Scope: preventive strategies, risk reduction, screening programs, and patient counseling.

Assign D8 when the item asks about: how to prevent a disease, patient education strategies, screening recommendations, or lifestyle modification counseling.

Examples:
- "What dietary advice reduces the risk of recurrent aphthous ulcers?"
- "Describe an oral cancer screening protocol for high-risk patients."
- "How would you counsel a patient about smoking cessation to reduce oral cancer risk?"

### D9: Systemic-Oral Correlations

Scope: the bidirectional relationship between systemic diseases and oral manifestations.

Assign D9 when the item involves: oral manifestations of systemic diseases (diabetes, HIV, autoimmune conditions), systemic effects of oral diseases, or medication side effects manifesting in the oral cavity.

Examples:
- "What oral manifestations are associated with Crohn's disease?"
- "How does uncontrolled diabetes affect periodontal disease progression?"
- "Which medications commonly cause drug-induced gingival overgrowth?"

### D10: Evidence-Based Practice and Research

Scope: appraising clinical evidence, understanding study design, applying guidelines, and recognizing knowledge gaps.

Assign D10 when the item asks about: interpreting study results, evaluating guideline recommendations, understanding levels of evidence, or identifying areas needing further research.

Examples:
- "Evaluate the level of evidence supporting the use of photodynamic therapy for oral leukoplakia."
- "A Cochrane review found insufficient evidence for a treatment. How does this affect your clinical decision?"
- "What study design would best evaluate a new oral cancer screening tool?"

### Dimension Co-Assignment Rules

- A question may receive up to 3 dimension tags.
- Always assign the primary dimension first, then secondary dimensions in relevance order.
- If a clinical vignette asks "What is the diagnosis AND how would you treat it?", tag both D1/D5 and D6.
- Co-assign D9 whenever a systemic condition is integral to the question, even if the primary dimension is something else.

## Subspecialties (S1–S10)

### S1: Oral Mucosal Diseases
Conditions affecting the oral mucosa: lichen planus, pemphigus, pemphigoid, aphthous stomatitis, leukoplakia, erythroplakia, oral candidiasis, herpetic stomatitis, and other mucosal lesions.

### S2: Periodontics
Diseases of the periodontium: gingivitis, chronic and aggressive periodontitis, necrotizing periodontal diseases, periodontal-systemic links, and periodontal treatment.

### S3: Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery
Surgical conditions and procedures: tooth extraction complications, odontogenic infections, jaw fractures, TMJ surgery, orthognathic surgery, and surgical management of oral pathology.

### S4: Oral and Maxillofacial Pathology
Histopathological diagnosis: odontogenic and non-odontogenic cysts and tumors, salivary gland pathology, bone pathology, and soft tissue tumors of the oral region.

### S5: Oral Radiology and Imaging
Diagnostic imaging: intraoral and extraoral radiography, CBCT, MRI, ultrasound interpretation, and radiographic features of oral diseases.

### S6: Dental Pharmacology and Therapeutics
Drugs used in oral medicine: analgesics, antibiotics, antifungals, antivirals, corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, local anesthetics, and drug interactions relevant to dental practice.

### S7: Pediatric Oral Medicine
Oral conditions in children: developmental anomalies, pediatric mucosal diseases, eruption disorders, and age-specific management considerations.

### S8: Geriatric Oral Medicine
Oral health in elderly patients: xerostomia, medication-related oral conditions, oral manifestations of age-related systemic diseases, and management considerations for geriatric patients.

### S9: Oral Oncology
Malignant and premalignant conditions: oral squamous cell carcinoma, verrucous carcinoma, salivary gland malignancies, lymphomas, staging, and oncologic management.

### S10: Temporomandibular Disorders and Orofacial Pain
TMJ disorders, myofascial pain, neuropathic orofacial pain, headache disorders with orofacial manifestations, and bruxism.

### Subspecialty Assignment Rules

- Assign the single most specific subspecialty. A histopathology question about oral lichen planus → S1 (disease context dominates), NOT S4.
- Assign a second subspecialty only when the item genuinely requires knowledge from both fields (e.g., surgical management of oral cancer → S3 + S9).
- When a systemic disease causes oral manifestations, assign based on the oral manifestation type (e.g., diabetes-related periodontitis → S2).

## Difficulty Levels (L1–L4)

### L1: Basic

Target audience: dental students in preclinical years.

Criteria:
- Tests recall of foundational facts taught in didactic courses.
- Single-concept questions with no clinical ambiguity.
- The correct answer is unambiguous and universally agreed upon.
- Distractors are clearly distinguishable from the correct answer.

Example: "Which cranial nerve provides sensory innervation to the anterior two-thirds of the tongue?"

### L2: Intermediate

Target audience: clinical dental students and recent graduates.

Criteria:
- Requires application of knowledge to a straightforward clinical scenario.
- The clinical presentation is typical/classic.
- One or two reasoning steps are needed.
- Distractors are plausible but distinguishable with adequate clinical knowledge.

Example: "A 35-year-old patient presents with painful, recurrent oral ulcers, genital ulcers, and uveitis. What is the most likely diagnosis?"

### L3: Advanced

Target audience: practicing dentists and oral medicine specialists.

Criteria:
- Requires integration of multiple knowledge domains.
- The clinical presentation may be atypical or involve complicating factors.
- Multiple reasoning steps are needed.
- Distractors are highly plausible and require nuanced differentiation.
- May involve rare conditions or complex management decisions.

Example: "A 55-year-old patient on long-term bisphosphonate therapy presents with exposed necrotic bone in the posterior mandible following extraction 8 weeks ago. Cultures show Actinomyces. What is the most appropriate initial management?"

### L4: Expert

Target audience: oral medicine specialists and academic clinicians.

Criteria:
- Requires expert-level synthesis across multiple domains.
- Involves diagnostic uncertainty, conflicting evidence, or evolving guidelines.
- May require evaluating evidence quality or reasoning about edge cases.
- The "correct" answer may involve acknowledging uncertainty or weighing trade-offs.
- Often maps to K4 (Metacognitive) knowledge level.

Example: "A recent meta-analysis suggests topical tacrolimus is non-inferior to topical corticosteroids for erosive OLP, but included studies have high heterogeneity and small sample sizes. How do you counsel a patient with steroid-resistant erosive OLP?"

### Difficulty Calibration Rules

- Difficulty is determined by cognitive demand on the target audience, NOT by disease rarity.
- A question about a common disease can be L4 if it requires expert-level reasoning.
- A question about a rare disease can be L1 if it only requires recalling its name.
- When uncertain between two levels, assign the higher level.
